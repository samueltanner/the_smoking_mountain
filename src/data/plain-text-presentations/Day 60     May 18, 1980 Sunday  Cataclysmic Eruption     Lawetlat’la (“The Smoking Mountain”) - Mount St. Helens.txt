Day 60     May 18, 1980  Sunday
Here is the day in order in a whole bunch of photographs, some that are famous and others more obscure…

The preceding 12 photographs of the earthquake and start of the landslide were taken by Dorothy and Keith Stoffel (save for the two photos from Mount Rainier). Dorothy was a geologist working for Century West Engineering Corporation in Spokane, and Keith was a geologist with the Division of Geology and Earth Resources at the Cheney field office. They drove to Yakima where Keith was going to represent the Division at the Yakima Gem and Mineral Show. On Sunday morning, May 18, with several hours to spend before the show, they decided to charter a private plane and fly to Mount St. Helens for pictures and observations. While nearly all Division geologists had flown around the volcano at least once during the previous two months, this was to be the Stoffel’s first trip.  
I had read parts of their story, but I came across the whole thing in this circular from the Washington State Dept. of Natural Resources. It's a neat old publication. The link is here and below…

Information Circular 71: The 1980 Eruption of Mount St. Helens, Washington, Part I
The 1980 Eruption of Mount St. Helens, Washington March 20 - May 19, 1980 
by Michael A. Korosec, James G. Rigby, and Keith L. Stoffel 
Information Circular 71 June 1980

• Next is another famous photograph, taken by Robert Rogers from the west side of the volcano in the Goat Mountain area (Road 8117). The first block of the landslide is rapidly descending and the black lateral pyroclastic surge beginning. †1  Rogers had climbed Mount St. Helens twice since it awoke in late March. He ran into Otto Sieber, Andy Sterne, and Brian Witt in mid-April as they were climbing and filming Keeper of the Fire  (see story and movie link in Day 55  May 13, 1980 post). On April 24, Rogers was climbing the west flank again when a USGS mapping crew gave him a lift, a helicopter ride to the summit. After explorations, he waded pumice and glissaded all the way down.  

• Climbing teams on Mount Rainier took series of photographs and described seeing the lateral pyroclastic surge following the topography perfectly as it spread, climbing ridges and descending into the valleys for about 6 minutes before being stopped by a strong north wind. †2

• The lahars on the volcano’s west and east flanks almost immediately scoured their channels as the South Fork Toutle River and the Muddy River and its tributaries saw very high velocities and superelevations (run-up). †3

• The Plinian Column was ascending above 80,000 feet by 0900 hours. The Plinian Column vented unabated for nine hours, beginning a decrease in intensity at 1730 hours.

• The first witnessed pyroclastic flows were at 1215 hours. Here is a spectacular flow sweeping down the west flank at 1217, soon to engulf snowy Crescent (West) Ridge. †4 

• The massive North Fork Toutle River lahar began forming in the upper valley at about 1300 hours, and the Cowlitz River crested at midnight, steamy hot and 92ºF. at Castle Rock. †5

[This is the Bob Fleming eruption day story. Bob has been a friend and colleague for 35 years, and his story is a classic.] 

Here ya go,   from the good Bob Book..........
1980    me and the girl friend leave IL and head west to end up at her father’s here in Lacey.    Got my 1974 Oldsmobile 98 loaded down and a pull behind U-haul trailer.  On the back of that trailer was their saying at the time,  An Adventure In Moving.    That ended up being so true.  Everything that could go wrong, went wrong on that trip.  But somehow we made it here.
 
Once out of Iowa head headed north and got the I-90 and came across.
Montana    in the middle of the night we broke down outside of Billings.  Limped into town   after searching the ditch for water to put in the radiator as we overheated.  Waiting until a store opened to buy a part.  A hose blew and caused us much grief.  But I was able to repair and get us going again.  Giddy up I believe is what was appropriate in MT.  Back on 90 and continued west until almost to Butte.  We are on top of some mountain and could see Butte down in the hole.   POP !    there goes my drivers side rear tire.  On top,  looking down the steep incline at the hole.  Pull over on what shoulder there was.  Remember, drivers side.  The jack and spare are buried in that trunk.  Removed everything to include album crates.  As a joke and making light of the situation I toss the tire out of the trunk.  Well,   it bounces.   doink doink doink,  I leap from the trunk and begin chasing my tire, now rolling down the hill.  Tackle !   Bring it back up.    That was close, almost lost my good tire.  Try to jack up with trailer attached.  Nope,  everything wants to go down the hill as the rear wheels left the pavement.

Now I am searching for a big rock to block my tires.  Found One !   Once airborn and dealing with things, my hiney is out in the road and every truck that went by made everything sway and wobble.  Yeeish......  scary moments right there.
Successful change and reload everything, and on down the hill to a station to repair the tire.  Re-unload and got patched.  Put everything back again.  I am getting to be a pro at this now.   But back on 90 and continue west.
Listening to the radio, beebopping along    merrily merrily life is but a dream.  Sun coming up behind us.  Having a good time.  And all of a sudden   we are about to enter Idaho, and the radio goes,   "Mt. St. Helens just erupted."   What ?   What ?
Did you hear that ??????   Pull into Spokane some place and found a pay phone.
I am standing outside with a little rain shelter cover over me and a phone on a wooden pole.    "Hey Jack (future father in-law),   we heard Helens just erupted, what do we do ?"   He says,   “you think you can beat it?”   
“What?  Beat what?”  I am in Spokane and no idea where we are other than that.  None of what is ahead do we know.   I say,   “I dunno,  we barely know we are at.”   He says,   “try and beat it.”  Still not knowing what IT is.   Unknowingly   I am brushing slight amounts of dust or something off of me while talking on that phone.  grrrr   Click - I hang up and tell the significant other    he says to try and beat it.   It again.........  So back in the car and back on 90 and away we go,   west still.    Yep,  things go worse as we went. Soon it was dark and blowing hard.   What the heck ????    ASH !   It was falling ash.  Struggled to Ellensburg.  Where stopped and got fuel and air filter, and another phone call.  Jack, we are in Ellensburg.   just so ya know.  This sucks.
Back on 90 and away we go again.  By now my car is covered with ash residue and inside is also    gritty.  It's everywhere and everything.   Head for the pass.  Up and over we go and scream down the other side.  Exhausted

from what we just went through   and it's still happening.  Nasty....    Oh   hey, it's I-5 and we go south.
We made it to south Tacoma way  or just about    when every light on the dash comes on warning us something ain't right and now no power steering.  Limp off into a gas station on South Tac way and near Galloping Gertie’s.  It's like 2 or 3 AM. Back on the phone.  “Jack,  my car died.  Has issues and I can't see much under the hood.”  He rushes up from Lacey,  first telling us to get back in the car and lock the doors.  I guess we were not in a positive part of town.  So we waited, he shows up,  finds us, discover what broke this time and we need parts.  Well,  not at 4 AM   so we head to his place, leaving everything behind.  Shut eyes briefly until stores open and we get parts.  An alternator and all the belts.  That alt seized up (from the ash we just drove through), and tossed all the belts, cooling, power steering, AC etc.      Back up we go,  I made repairs and and top off fluids and away we go,  Lacey bound.   Once here and parked,   my car was a mess.  Inside, outside, even in the trailer.  That powder was on everything and in my eyes.   I was 19 at the time and wondering what the heck just happened.  The safest cleanest place was inside that trunk.  lol 
My albums survived.  sheeewwwwwwww   I never did get all the ash form that car.  Every crack, crevice, vent duct work etc   you name it had a layer of ash until the day she was put to rest in 1988ish  give or take a yr.  
I-5 claimed her again in a 20 car pile up under the Dupont underpass, in a nasty rain storm.  Flooded under the pass     where a Volkswagen hydroplaned and came up and over the center barrier and into our lane.  Well at 60, things happened fast boom boom boom etc.   That 98 PLOWED into that growing pile of cars, and I was like the 4th or 5th one in that line.  I hit that little Honda in front of me so hard, it was like a watermelon seed.  Spit out and headed for the overpass embankment.  That's another story,   but that 98 was the ONLY ONE to be able to still drive away from the scene once the state patrol released me.  Everyone else was getting the hook or towed 

 
in some way.  I limped home again.   She then became a toy for the kids to play in.  Hood was pretty kinked and bent front fender.  Still ran and drove or moved but was hard to see over the hood.  Went shopping for a new car.   Bamm.
 
So I always tell everyone, U-haul truly is an adventure in moving and that Mt. St. Helens welcomed me to Washington.
That's my story and I am sticking to it.

Beginning May 26, we’ll spend three days discussing the Debris Avalanche. Succeeding days will cover the Pyroclastic Density Current (lateral pyroclastic surge), the Blast Pyroclastic Flows, the Plinian Column and ashfall, the Pumiceous Pyroclastic Flows, and the Lahars. Topics then turn to the new and affected lakes…Coldwater Lake, South Fork Castle Creek Lake, Spirit Lake, Saint Helens Lake, Fawn Lake, Venus Lake, and the other blast zone lakes. Plus, stories about superelevations of high-velocity lahars, the volcano’s missing glaciers, Crescent Ridge, and tales of friends and colleagues.
The subsequent summer and fall 1980 eruptions and spectacular pyroclastic flows out of the new amphitheater deserve their own slide shows too (May 25, June 12, July 22, August 7, and October 16-18).
