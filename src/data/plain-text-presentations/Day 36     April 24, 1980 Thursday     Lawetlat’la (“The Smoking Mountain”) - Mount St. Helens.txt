Day 36     April 24, 1980  Thursday
The Al Raught Bridge carried Spirit Lake Highway 504 over the North Fork Toutle River. The span was located 23 miles downstream of Spirit Lake between Weyerhaeuser's Camp Baker and the town of Kid Valley (map below). The bridge’s green steel girder construction was akin to almost every bridge spanning Western Washington’s many rivers, but very few of the old bridges remain today. The Al Raught Bridge became internationally famous less than one month after today’s first picture was snapped, as millions viewed photographs of the bridge in a brand-new location.   

USGS daily report
Although fumaroles continued to emit steam near the summit crater, no ash eruptions were large enough to throw material outside the crater walls. The number of earthquakes was about the same as it has been in recent days, but the quakes were stronger. Eight were of magnitude 4 or greater, and one reached magnitude 4.7. The USGS Volcanic Hazards Project made public a map showing the possible dangers presented by the bulge area and emphasized the potential for avalanches.   [USGS PP 1249]

After driving over all of those bridges as they crossed back and forth across the Main Branch Toutle River and the North Fork, excited travelers on Spirit Lake Highway 504 would find Harry Truman’s Mt. St. Helens Lodge, the idyllic Spirit Lake campground, and Duck Bay. 

C. Glen Jorgensen  Former Gifford Pinchot Forest Supervisor (1957-1961) noted…
Road 99 is of special interest. It was planned not only to manage the timber resource, but also to provide alternate access to Spirit Lake making loop trips possible. S.R.# 504 connected with Road 99 beyond Timberline, and then with Iron Creek Road 25. Along the Spirit Lake/Smith Creek divide the road was located so that it could not be seen from anywhere in the Spirit Lake basin. A timber screen was maintained in all the saddles.
