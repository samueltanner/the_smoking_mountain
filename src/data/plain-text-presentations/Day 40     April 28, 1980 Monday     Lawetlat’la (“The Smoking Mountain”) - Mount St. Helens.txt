Day 40     April 28, 1980  Monday
USGS daily report
Although poor visibility hampered aerial observations, there was no indication today that any eruptions of ash were large enough to extend beyond the crater. When the crater was visible, it was emitting clouds of steam punctuated by occasional short bursts of ash. The northern flank continues to deform, as the tiltmeter at Timberline indicates. Most scientists are unwilling to specify the exact cause of the tilting, but they generally agree that it probably is related to the subterranean movement of magma.   ​[USGS PP 1249]

Historic photographs

The Dog’s Head route was easily the most popular climbing route up the volcano, beginning at the Timberline parking lot. I have many favorite pre-eruption photographs, but the next one is surely on the very top of the pile and I never tire of looking at it. 

The Dog’s Head is a dacite dome and was formed during the Castle Creek eruptive period (2200 to 1600 years ago). During this period, basaltic lava flows also created the Ape Caves and Lava Canyon on the southeast flank.
