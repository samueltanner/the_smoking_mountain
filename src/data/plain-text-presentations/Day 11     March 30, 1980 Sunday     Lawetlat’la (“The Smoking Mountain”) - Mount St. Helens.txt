Day 11     March 30, 1980  Sunday
USGS daily report
The first major eruption of the day was reported at 0410 hours. By 0740, a huge anvil-shaped cloud of steam and ash had formed. Drifting to the southeast, it produced light ashfall miles away.  
Observers counted 93 eruptions of steam and ash from the volcano today. Five eruptions sent ash more than one mile above the twin craters. Last night, airborne observers could see a flickering blue flame burning within the older crater or jumping from crater to crater. Possible flammable gases were hydrogen, hydrogen sulfide, methane, or carbon monoxide, the most likely being hydrogen sulfide. [The flame disappeared on April 2 before scientists could determine the exact composition of the gas.] Burning gas has been observed previously above lava flows and lava lakes in other volcanic regions but not in craters before the appearance of lava. Seismographs at the University of Washington recorded 58 earthquakes of magnitudes greater than 3.0, including 6 above 4.0. 
In the afternoon, as many as 70 aircraft reportedly were flying around the volcano at the same time, and strict air-traffic control was necessary to prevent aerial collisions.   [USGS PP 1249]
