Day 77     June 4, 1980  Wednesday   

• One of my very favorite pictures of two very favorite people, Jimmy Poole on the Muddy River Boulder and Rod Williams below. If you were at Colleen's and my wedding, you likely met them both. Jimmy is surveying the high mud marks left on the riverbank trees, and they’ll return later to run elevations to the boulder. †1
• Pre- and post-eruption aerials of Fawn Lake, nine miles NW of the volcano, on May 17 and June 4, 1980. The “before” photograph was taken just in time. Amazing things happened at Fawn Lake the next morning, and will be discussed in a later presentation. †2 
• Smith Creek valley on the eastern side of Mount St. Helens was heavily impacted by the pyroclastic density current, lahars, blast pyroclastic flows, and late fall floods with the first heavy rains.
The tributary waterfalls to Smith Creek are spectacular, and presently the lower falls in section 29 can be seen from far distances when hiking on the higher slopes near Windy Ridge. The lower falls drops 216 feet. I included the map because I just noticed the elevations of the top and bottom of each falls are printed. I’ve never seen that before on a topographic sheet. †3 
• The surface water level of Spirit Lake receded in the first couple weeks after the eruption despite being dammed by the debris avalanche deposit. Water was seeping out of the debris dam in places and some of the very warm lake water may have evaporated. The normally deep late spring snowpack had vanished and the lake's inlets were dry. The receding water surface is clearly marked in the photograph by the ash line at the base of Harry's Ridge. †4

USGS daily report
The volcano's plumes continued to be principally steam, which rose regularly to altitudes of 12,000 to 14,000 feet. Earthquake activity was low. Harmonic tremor, which yesterday was stronger than it has been at any time since May 25th, decreased in amplitude. Upward (inflationary) tilting on the volcano's southern side continues at a very slow rate, quite unlike the rapid bulging of the northern side before the May 18 eruption. Scientists continue to warn that the apparent quiet may be misleading. The volcano may be poised for another eruption. Despite the continuing apprehension of scientists, however, the Red Zone is scheduled to reopen today for loggers, residents, and news reporters if the volcano remains quiet. 
A hospital patient died today of burns received during the May 18 eruption. A body discovered about 3 miles west of the crater was identified as that of a missing Portland photographer, Robert Landsburg.   [USGS PP 1249]

Pumiceous Pyroclastic Flows
The Plinian Column produced numerous Pumiceous Pyroclastic Flows on May 18. The first pyroclastic flow was seen at 1215-1217 hours. Most of the pyroclastic flows raced out through the new north-facing breach of the crater and were emplaced in succession over the debris avalanche deposit, some flowing into Spirit Lake and others flowing down the North Fork Toutle River valley. As the eruption column expanded at the rim of Mount St. Helens’ new amphitheater, gravity generated thin ash flows that spread out over the remaining upper slopes of the volcano. Many flows were photographed descending the west, southwest, and east flanks. Pyroclastic flows continued at least until dark. 
Pumiceous pyroclastic flow temperatures on May 18 were 572-1346ºF.  Flow temperatures did not decrease substantially along their path downslope. Deposits near the volcano’s vent were emplaced at 1382-1562ºF. The May 18 deposits thicken downslope, measuring three feet thick 1.5-2.5 miles from the vent, and as much as 130 feet thick 2.5-5 miles away. 
Significant magmatic eruptions and pumiceous pyroclastic flows occurred on May 25, June 12, July 22, August 7, and October 16-18, 1980, and had even higher initial temperatures than the May 18 flow.
