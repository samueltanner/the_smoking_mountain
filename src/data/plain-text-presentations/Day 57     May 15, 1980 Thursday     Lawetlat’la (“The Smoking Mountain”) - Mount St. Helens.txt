Day 57     May 15, 1980  Thursday

USGS daily report
The volcano was visible part of the day. USGS observers saw no eruptions, but they could see new ash on the southwestern flank. A few of the summit vents appear to be filled with fallen blocks of ice. The north-side bulge continues to shift outward at a rate of about five feet a day. Seismic activity included one earthquake of magnitude 4.8 early this morning.

Mount St. Helens continues to attract the curious from around the world. Hard-pressed law-enforcement officers, unable to man roadblocks on the maze of logging roads around the mountain, can only check periodically for people trying to get around the barricades and locked gates for a closer look at the volcano. Many roadblocks have become places where visitors congregate, picnicking, socializing, photographing, or just waiting for the clouds to lift and for the volcano to "do something." Their vehicles often clog the very routes that they would need to escape an explosive eruption.

Fifty days have now passed since the March 27 eruption, but no lava has been seen, and no evidence of fresh magma has been found in the ash. Even with the renewal of ash eruptions on May 7, the amount of tephra ejected since March 27 has not been great in comparison with that ejected during eruptions of other volcanoes in the Cascade Range or during previous eruptions of Mount St. Helens itself. May 15, 1980 Thursday TNT news clipping

May 15, 1980  On the northern end of Spirit Lake’s East Arm, the Portland YMCA's Camp Meehan holds about $40,000 in gear. A mile away, the Boy Scouts’ camp holds $30,000 of equipment. Favors are asked, and an expedition is organized to retrieve gear so it can be used at other camps. Governor Dixy Lee Ray signs a temporary exception to the executive order closing access to the Red Zone. Tents, life jackets, backpacks, kitchen gear, sailboats, fire pumps, climbing ropes, generators, tools, aluminum skiffs – literally tons of gear – are relayed by Columbia Helicopters across the lake and transported out. The media is told of the good deeds done and the stories run, nettling owners who have been trying to visit their own cabins. They have been threatened by $500 fines and 6 months jail time if they venture into the Red Zone. But now a group is allowed to retrieve gear? Cabin owners dial the sheriff and eventually arrange their own trip on Saturday May 17.
