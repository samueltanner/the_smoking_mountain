Day 10     March 29, 1980  Saturday
USGS daily report
The weather cleared today, and airborne observers noted a second, larger crater which apparently opened during the night. The new opening measured about 75 feet wide and 30 to 60 feet deep. Sporadic eruptions of steam and ash were now coming mainly from this new crater. Clouds of erupted ash and steam that swept slowly down the mountain sometimes were accompanied by lightning flashes, some of them two miles long. Scientists speculated that the ground-hugging lightning bolts were caused by rock particles in the ash cloud rubbing together to produce electrostatic discharges. The first USGS analyses of volcanic ash showed that the ash is composed of pulverized old rock propelled into the air by steam eruptions. There was no evidence of "juvenile" rock material that would indicate the presence of magma at shallow depth in the volcano. The explosive eruptions to date are phreatic eruptions, as subsurface water flashes violently into steam when contacting very hot rocks that are being heated by the magma beneath.
86 earthquakes were recorded today that exceeded magnitude 3, included 39 ranging in magnitude from 3.5 to 4.4. 
Field crews installed the first tiltmeter station, which will help to measure the surface deformation of the volcano. USGS hydrologists continue to monitor the streams closely but have detected no unusual increases in streamflow.  [USGS PP 1249]

TNT news clippings 

Historic photographs
After the Yacolt Burn south of Mount St. Helens in 1902 and gigantic forest fires in Idaho and Montana in 1910,   the U.S. Forest Service turned toward suppression. Columbia National Forest built a fire lookout on the summit of Mount St. Helens in summer 1921, with Al Robbins and three carpenters hauling every board, window, nail and cable all the way up. They would start out at four in the morning, bake eggs in the steam jets at The Boot at 8000 feet, and summit with the day’s load by nine. No Gore-Tex, Patagonia Puffies or Jet Boils for those rangers. The lookout was abandoned due to frequent poor visibility and boarded up a decade plus later in favor of lower summits. The last lookout was Ken Macdonald, in July/August 1926 and 1927. 
The Strawberry Mountain (12 miles NE from the volcano), Vanson Peak (15 miles NNE), and French Butte 
(16 miles NE) fire lookouts are also long gone.
