Day 58     May 16, 1980  Friday

USGS daily report   The lull in eruptions continues, and seismicity has remained at about the same level for the past few days. Today, there were 28 earthquakes stronger than magnitude 3.0; ten reached 4.0 or greater. No harmonic tremor has been recorded since May 8. Airborne observers could see clearly into the growing summit crater and noted that steam continues to emanate from the summit area, particularly from a large fumarole on the southern wall and from a gradually enlarging pit at the head of Shoestring Glacier. The bulging northern side was also steaming today, especially on its higher parts. The volcano has changed shape considerably during the last week and a half. The northern and northwestern rims of the crater show abundant cracks, which have been partly filled by snow and ash. This area appears to be moving downward as a mass toward the crater. The bulge area also continues to be highly broken and distorted.

Before dawn today, an aerial thermal survey of the volcano was made by the U.S. Department of Energy. Because the results of the survey were recorded as digital data on tape, they are not immediately available for interpretation. Glaciologists set up a time-lapse camera on Dogs Head, one mile northeast of the mountain's crest, and aimed it at the growing bulge. The camera takes a picture every half hour and can operate for more than five days without being reloaded. Cabins and camps at Spirit Lake at the foot of the volcano, have been deserted for the most part as they are in the Red Zone. Yesterday and today, however, workers were allowed to remove equipment from the Boy Scout and YMCA camps along the lake. Owners of private property in the no entry zones are demanding similar access to the homes and cabins they were forced to leave weeks ago. Some 

threatened to converge in numbers on the roadblocks and go through "come hell or high water." The penalty for being found in the Red Zone without permission has been set at $500 or a 6-month jail sentence. The National Weather Service predicts good weather for volcano watching this weekend. David and Brian Cole’s Deadmans Lake adventure
The Cole brothers jumped in their truck today, bound for Deadmans Lake with their Irish Setter, Bridgette. 
David, 26, of Forks, and Brian, 23, of Centralia, drove logging roads to the Vanson Peak trailhead. They ambled  four miles to Deadmans, 12.7 miles NNE of Mount St. Helens. The lake sits high above the Green River valley, just to the north and just below Goat Mountain and its long ridgeline. Finding a well-used hunter’s camp, David and Brian built a big fire and prepared for the next day’s adventures. 
Five of their May 16, 1980 photograph follow. Tomorrow's May 17 presentation includes many more, and more of their story…

Deformation map of Mount St. Helens’ bulge on north flank

The link below is to a really neat map, showing the volcano’s changes between April 11 and May 16, 1980.
By the latter date, the giant bulge on Mount St. Helens' north flank had deformed 480 feet outward. The bulge was primarily at 8400-8800 feet in elevation at a point nearly due north on the volcano.
The first map legend discusses how the map was created; the second legend explains the color-coding system used for the map contours; and the third shows the deformation in profile. 
This map deserves being studied on a computer, as the details might be lost and confusing on a phone’s or tablet’s small format.

Historic Photograph
Mary and Ron Rinear and their children drove down to their cabin at Maple Flats (Kid Valley) on the North Fork of the Toutle River on Friday May 16, 1980. They had originally planned to stay the whole weekend, but instead had to drive back to Lacey on Saturday the 17th for Ron to run a work-a-thon fundraiser in the late afternoon for girl’s athletics at North Thurston High School.

By chance, the Rinear’s were not at their cabin Sunday morning, May 18.
