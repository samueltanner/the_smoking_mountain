Day 12     March 31, 1980  Monday
USGS daily report
Shifting winds sent ash from the volcano's frequent eruptions toward different, more densely populated areas today. By noon, the Kelso/Longview area was lightly dusted by ash. No juvenile rock material has been seen in the ash during the recent eruptions. Airborne observers reported that the twin craters are both growing and have nearly merged. The frequency of earthquakes near the volcano decreased, yet the average magnitude increased. 
Tourists, who had been disappointed by bad weather last week and roadblocks around the mountain, have been regularly skirting the closure boundary during the good weather at the end of March. Bill Stewart of the Vancouver Columbian wrote, "It appeared that there was a competition to see who could get closest. Some people actually landed in helicopters on the crater rim and climbed inside. One group wearing camouflage clothing climbed to the summit and filmed scenes intended for a documentary movie and for beer commercials.” [USGS PP 1249]

Historic photographs 
The U.S. Forest Service Spirit Lake Guard Station (built in 1910) and Ranger Station (1913), the Mt. St. Helens Lodge, Harmony Falls Lodge, Portland (1909) and Longview YMCA camps, Girl & Boy Scout camps, and church camps were all built on Spirit Lake by 1950, mainly on the south shore and along the East Arm. Camp Meehan, as the Portland YMCA Camp was known, moved across the lake to Paradise Creek at the head of the East Arm in 1946. The Spirit Lake Lodge, one mile downstream on the North Fork Toutle River, was built around 1941 by Harry and Julia (Lange) Gustafson on the Lange homestead. They advertised souvenirs, home-cooked meals, country-style fried chicken, and huckleberry pies.
