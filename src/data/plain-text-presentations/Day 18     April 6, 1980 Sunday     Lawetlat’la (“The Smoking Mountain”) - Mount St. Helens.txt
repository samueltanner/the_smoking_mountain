Day 18     April 6, 1980  Sunday
USGS daily report
Although clouds, rain, snow, and occasional "mud rains" containing ash hampered visibility today, they also kept away much of the expected crowd of sightseers. The Spirit Lake area received 6 inches of new snow. Earthquake activity around Mount St. Helens was about the same as it has been in the last few days - hundreds of quakes of magnitude 3 or less, 54 greater than magnitude 3, and 6 of magnitude 4.0 or greater. No harmonic tremor was recorded today, however. Scientists found the ash to be 3 inches thick on the eastern flank of the mountain at the 5,000-foot level. In an interview with Seattle Times reporter Hill Williams, USGS geologist Dwight "Rocky" Crandell explained that scientists really do not know what to expect from the volcano because information about actual eruptions of Cascade Range volcanoes is limited and because the eruptive history of Mount St. Helens is different from that of other Cascade volcanoes. "Mount St. Helens has done so many different things in the past that hardly anything would be a surprise," Crandell said. 'The only thing it hasn't done is blow itself apart."    [USGS PP 1249]

Historic photographs
The Mount Margaret Backcountry (Gifford Pinchot National Forest) lies north by northeast of the volcano and Spirit Lake, ranging from six to 12 miles from the volcano. Mount Whittier sits in the middle of the backcountry at the head of Coldwater Creek, a steep and rocky 2-mile-long spine.  
The beautiful alpland around Saint Helens Lake, The Dome, and Mount Margaret is more friendly these days and ready for high country wandering. 
Four volcanoes are in sight...Rainier, Adams, Hood, and St. Helens. A string of eight distinctly individual lakes lie behind Mounts Whittier and Margaret. Once very popular with horsemen, fishermen, hikers and Scouts, the post-eruption Mount Margaret Backcountry area has certainly been rediscovered.
