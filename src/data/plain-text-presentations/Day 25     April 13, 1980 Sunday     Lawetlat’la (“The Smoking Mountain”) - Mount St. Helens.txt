USGS daily report
Between sunrise and sunset today, USGS scientists counted 18 eruptions of steam and ash ranging in duration from a few minutes to 45 minutes. The recording tiltmeter installed in the parking lot at Timberline Viewpoint showed small but consistent changes. The overall results indicate a slight subsidence of the central part of the volcano relative to the Timberline instrument station. To monitor surface tilt more closely, the USGS installed an additional tiltmeter on East Dome at the foot of Ape Glacier. The number of earthquakes of magnitude 4 or greater increased slightly today; there were nine, the largest number since March 29. Clear weather brought record-breaking numbers of sightseers to the area from all over the United States and from other countries. This weekend, two television camera crews were airlifted onto the mountain in defiance of closure orders, and unauthorized climbers were also sighted. Forest Service officials warned again that the mountain is not safe and that it is off limits to the press and public above the 4,400-foot level.   [USGS PP 1249]

Historic photographs
When including the bathymetric map of Fawn Lake in the Saturday April 10 post, I forgot a very important part...photographs of Leonard Nelson and Norm Dion from the Tacoma USGS Water Resources Division office. Leonard, Norm, Gil Bortleson, Jim Innes, and Jim McConnell were involved in a Washington lakes inventory from 1974-1976 and performed water quality and bathymetric surveys all over Washington, including Fawn, Venus, and Spirit Lakes near Mount St. Helens. 
Venus Lake and Lower Venus Lake, 10 and 10.4 miles NNE of the volcano, were studied in September 1974. Venus Lake is 20 acres in surface area and supported Rainbow trout; Lower Venus 8 acres. 140 feet deep, Venus Lake was exceptionally clear, with a 20-centimeter black and white target (a Secchi disc) visible to a 66-foot depth. 
A few years ago (2020), I climbed and bushwhacked to the ridge above Snow Lake until Big Venus Lake was revealed. It is a gem. Someday, I will return and drop down to hang out and swim on its beautiful, open western shoreline and beach. 
Norm’s pre-eruption photograph of Venus Lake follows and it’s a beauty. I first saw it in March 2023 when it was included in the 100+ photographs that Sandy Embrey sent me. I was so excited, since I had never seen a pre-eruption photo of Venus besides vertical aerials. You’ll see most of Sandy’s lakes pictures later, along with a perfect post-eruption of Venus Lake to match Norm’s.
