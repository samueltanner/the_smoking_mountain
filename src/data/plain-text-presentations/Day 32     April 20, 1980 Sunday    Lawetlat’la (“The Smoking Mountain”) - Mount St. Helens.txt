USGS daily report
No eruptions were seen today, but seismic activity increased somewhat. There were 47 earthquakes stronger than magnitude 3; seven were stronger than magnitude 4. This count was the highest in two weeks. Although clouds have covered the peak most of the weekend, tourists continue to be a problem. These first weeks of volcanic activity have been treated as a gigantic carnival by some people in the Northwest, who have come to see Mount St. Helens "performing" on center stage.
Tee shirts imprinted with various slogans, coffee cups bearing photographs, plastic bags filled with ash, and other souvenirs are being offered for sale. Sightseers line the roads leading to the mountain despite several "Mount St. Helens viewpoints" that the State of Washington has designated near Interstate Highway 5 so that the local roads leading to the mountain would be less congested. The opening of the sportfishing season this weekend doubtlessly contributed to the crowds in this popular fishing area. Understandably, a great many people want to see the first erupting volcano in the conterminous United States in more than half a century.   [USGS PP 1249]

Historic photographs
Spirit Lake’s limnology was first studied by fisheries biologists from the Washington State Department of Game in July 1937, and was classified ultra oligotrophic as the cold clear water produced little algae. The Game Department studied the lake’s capacity to sustain fish populations to supplement the small population of resident cutthroat and rainbow first planted in 1913. They found Spirit Lake high in water quality, along with an near absence of plankton and benthic invertebrates. Despite the low potential for fish sustainability, WDG stocked Spirit Lake with 40,000 legal-sized rainbow trout every year from 1951 until 1979. Lake recreational activities took place mainly in the summer months, and even then only five to ten anglers fished the lake daily. Anadromous coho steelhead, and sea-run cutthroat historically spawned in tributaries below Spirit Lake in the Toutle River drainage. 
All of the lakes in the vicinity of the Mount St. Helens were managed by the Department of Game for trout fishing and contained a mixture of eastern brook and cutthroat trout. Most lakes were planted by helicopter or plane on a three-year rotation basis. Fifty to 100 fish per acre was the norm, meaning that as many as 150,000 fish could have been in the lakes in 1980.
