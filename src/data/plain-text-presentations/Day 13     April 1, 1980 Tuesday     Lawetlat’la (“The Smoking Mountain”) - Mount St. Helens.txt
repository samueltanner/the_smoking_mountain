USGS daily report
Clouds shrouded the lower part of the mountain, but the top was clear enough for aerial observations. Several eruptions of ash and steam reached as high as 20,000 feet. 
Light ashfall was reported on the outskirts of Vancouver, and the village of Cougar (11 miles southwest of the volcano) received somewhat larger amounts. Some observers believe that these eruptions are the strongest since the volcano awoke in late March. USGS scientists reported that a huge graben (wedge) of ice-capped rocks in the crater area has settled at least 200 feet downward between the major cracks crossing the mountaintop. This settling has caused a noticeable outward displacement, or bulging, of the rocks and glacier ice on the upper north flank of Mount St. Helens. Airborne observers noted that the two summit craters are now essentially a single crater more than 600 feet across. At least two new steam vents also were spotted in the crater area.  
Last night and early this morning, seismographs recorded three of the strongest quakes to date - two of magnitude 4.6 and one of magnitude 4.7. This afternoon, another magnitude 4.6 quake occurred. Between 1925-1930 hours, the first weak harmonic tremor was noted on the seismograms at the University of Washington. It was an undulating ground movement of the kind typically associated with the underground movement of magma. Volcano experts are saying that the possibility of eruptions involving magma seems to be increasing.  

Their evidence includes: the stronger earthquakes accompanied by harmonic tremor; the new steam vents; and the presence of sulfur dioxide in the gas samples taken last Friday, which shows that a high temperature source for the gas exists within the mountain. Scientists are eager to get new vertical aerial photographs of the mountain to compare with similar vertical photographs taken in 1979. Comparison of the two sets of photographs will help to determine the physical changes that the mountain has undergone during the last few weeks. 
April 1, which usually marks the start of the mountain climbing season, finds Mount St. Helens closed to climbing. Spirit Lake, which averages 658,000 visitors annually, is also closed. Farther from the mountain, however, about 300 loggers have returned to their jobs around Mount St. Helens and at Camp Baker on the North Fork Toutle River. Officials of Cowlitz and Skamania Counties have asked the Washington National Guard to help maintain the roadblocks around the volcano. The restricted zone is not difficult to enter because the mountain is crisscrossed by dirt logging roads. One official estimated that as many as 29 barriers requiring about 180 officers would be needed to completely block the mountain roads.   [USGS PP 1249]

Historic photographs
Jack Nelson opened Nelson’s Boat House, a fishing camp, on Spirit Lake in summer 1926. Harry Truman snowshoed in the following winter to escape Prohibition-era gangsters that he had run afoul of with his liquor smuggling activities. Nelson renamed his place Mt. St. Helens Lodge in 1929, and partnered with Truman. Harry’s caustic personality quickly soured their relationship. They fought over how each treated the customers and over the rental boats. Nelson moved across the lake and with his wife, Tressa, built the idyllic Harmony Falls Lodge on the East Arm of Spirit Lake. 
Truman’s lodge burned, and in summer 1939 Truman and Harry Gardner built the new three-story Mt. St. Helens Lodge. In the 1940s, he added cabins, a restaurant, and a boathouse and dock filled with small craft.
Many cabins at Truman's resort were destroyed by a major storm in 1950. After Truman rebuilt, several cabins, the dock, and the huge surrounding trees were destroyed by the 80-150 mile-per-hour winds in the devastating Columbus Day storm on October 12, 1962.
