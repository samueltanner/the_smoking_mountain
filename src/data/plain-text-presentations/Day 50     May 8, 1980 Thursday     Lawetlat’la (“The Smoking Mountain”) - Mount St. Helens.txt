Day 50     May 8, 1980  Thursday

USGS daily report
Muddy rain fell on the Timberline monitoring station today, and ash blew to the north and east as the mountain continued its sporadic eruptions. The crater steamed, as did areas near the head of Shoestring Glacier and the Boot, high on the eastern and northern sides of the cone, respectively. Many geologists agree that it is only a matter of time before the bulge area fails, probably in a gigantic landslide. They are unable to judge, however, when the failure is likely to occur. The strength of the rocks under the bulge and the depth to which they are affected by the deformation are not known. In the meantime, the northern flank continues its outward creep. Earthquake frequency is about the same as it has been in previous weeks (25 stronger than magnitude 3). However, eleven quakes stronger than magnitude 4 were recorded - the largest number of higher magnitude quakes since April 24. The strongest of these registered 5.0, the most powerful single quake to date. Harmonic tremor returned to the records for the first time since April 12. Tremor was recorded in three bursts, from 0443 to 0503 and 1004 to 1013 hours in the morning, and 2003 to 2012 hours in the late evening.   [USGS PP 1249]

Historic Photographs
The Goat Rocks dacite dome was extruded and grew midway up the northwest flank between 1831-1857. 
It followed a voluminous pumice eruption and the Floating Island andesitic lava flow, dated to 1800 by growth rings of trees still living very near the lava. The growth of the Goat Rocks dome included glowing avalanches, and hot and cool mudflows. Many events were eyewitnessed, especially between 1842 and 1847. A fur trapper living on the Lewis River drainage reported that the light from a south flank eruption and lava flow on November 22, 1842 was so intense he was able to see and pick up a pin in his cabin at midnight. People also wrote of periodic darkness and haze from Mount St. Helens during the 26-year eruptive period. The Goat Rocks dome was the most easily identified landmark on the volcano, along with the Shoestring Glacier.
