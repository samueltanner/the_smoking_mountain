• Imagine that charred tree limb and evergreen cone flying 35 miles, all the way to Mount Adams! The climbers on Mount Rainier who took those great eruption day photographs of the spreading lateral pyroclastic surge were quite puzzled when charred twigs and needles floated down upon them, 50 miles from Mount St. Helens. †1 

Mount St. Helens' crater and amphitheater still won't show itself, three days after the eruption.  

• Less than one month before, Peter Lipman was piloting a little wooden boat from Harry Truman's Mt. St. Helens Lodge all the way to the head of the Spirit Lake’s East Arm and Camp Loowit. Today he is photographing Don Swanson taking a little stroll on the log-choked West Arm. And playing Superman with other geologists. †2 
• Smith Creek drains the east side of the volcano and is tributary to the Muddy River. The valley took a terrific beating with the pyroclastic density current, lahars, and an extensive blast pyroclastic deposit. The highest temperatures of the density current were measured in the eastern sector. †3

USGS daily report
Eruptive and seismic activity continues at a low level. The mountain vented only plumes of steam today, and only two earthquakes greater than magnitude 3 were recorded. Scientists flew to Spirit Lake and found the level to be about 150 feet below the top of the debris avalanche deposit. Although the danger of an outburst flood is thus lessened somewhat, they nevertheless placed markers along the lake to help maintain a watch on its level. 

The Federal Aviation Administration today warned flyers that volcanic ash can pock aircraft windshields and clog engines. The ash cloud in the stratosphere reached the Atlantic coast. Reporters in Yakima, after establishing a grid within the city and sampling and weighing ash from grid locations, determined that about 600,000 tons of ash from Sunday's eruption have fallen on the 12.9-square-mile city. Spokane and several smaller communities have been forced to ration city water because residents' attempts to wash ash from cars, streets, and buildings have depleted reservoirs. The City of Longview has instituted emergency water-use measures for another reason. Intake pipes from the Cowlitz River, which normally supply the city's reservoirs, have been clogged by mud and debris from the flooding that began on May 18. The mass of muddy debris so far has resisted all efforts to remove it from the intakes. Electrical utility crews in the "ash belt" are busy removing accumulations of ash from high-voltage powerline insulators by using high-pressure air and water jets. Although the ash is an insulator when it is dry, it conducts electricity when it is wet and causes high voltage arcing and power surges that damage electrical equipment. 
President Carter today declared Washington State a disaster area, and major Federal aid to victims of the
volcano's devastation can begin. The Coast Guard reported that 31 ocean bound ships now are tied up at  

Portland and Vancouver awaiting the reopening of the Columbia River shipping channel. Another ten ships are waiting at Astoria, Oregon to come to those upriver ports. Several incoming ships originally scheduled to call at Columbia River ports now have been diverted elsewhere. Thirty military helicopters scheduled to search today for survivors and victims of the catastrophe were grounded because of cloudy weather.   [USGS PP 1249]

Topics then turn to the new and affected lakes…Coldwater Lake, South Fork Castle Creek Lake, Spirit Lake, Saint Helens Lake, Fawn Lake, Venus Lake, and the other blast zone lakes. Plus, stories about superelevations of high-velocity lahars, the volcano’s missing glaciers, Crescent Ridge, and tales of friends and colleagues.
The subsequent summer and fall 1980 eruptions and spectacular pyroclastic flows out of the new amphitheater deserve their own slide shows too (May 25, June 12, July 22, August 7, and October 16-18).
