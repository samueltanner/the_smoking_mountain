• Today’ first photograph is a funny one. Looks like one of my little students digging on the playfield at recess. That is quite the giant ice ball he discovered. †1

• Photograph taken from atop one of largest hummocks in the debris avalanche deposit. The hummock came to rest near the confluences of the two Coldwater valleys with the North Fork Toutle River. The scientist is essentially pointing at the location where Coldwater Lake was already growing behind its blockage. He is superimposed on the toe of Coldwater Ridge. The up-valley view is of South Fork Coldwater Creek. The valley was completely stripped of all vegetation by the debris avalanche and the lateral pyroclastic surge, and then buried by an amazing blast pyroclastic deposit. South Coldwater (Johnston) Ridge is on the right. †2

USGS daily report 
The volcano ejected plumes of ash and steam about 15,000 feet above the crater twice today, at about 1430 and 2305 hours. Ash sprinkled towns as far south as Salem. Harmonic tremor, which has continued at varying weak levels throughout the past week, was somewhat stronger most of today. USGS scientists installed new targets on the mountain for laser distance measuring instruments today. They also measured temperatures of about 540°F (280°C) in pyroclastic flow deposits on the volcano's northern flank. Geologists mapping in the devastated area found that the lateral blast had carried stones as large as grapefruit that undoubtedly caused much of the stripping and battering done to the exposed surfaces of trees. About 45 National Guardsmen arrived today in Ritzville, which has received the heaviest ashfall of any community away from the volcano. They began shoveling ash from some of the town's flat roofs to reduce the danger of collapse.   [USGS PP 1249]

Note: Over the coming weeks, I will continue posting photographs that were taken on that day 45 years ago.
Many other photographs, mainly from 1980-1981, will illustrate different aspects of the May 18 eruption along with maps, research links, artwork, and newspaper clippings. Hopefully, many of the photographs will be new to you. I am especially excited about the beautiful black & whites taken by Jan Fardell from 1980-83, and photos taken by my USGS colleagues from the Water Resources Division in Tacoma. Many slides were hiding in closets in Kodak Carousel trays and have never been seen until now.

Beginning May 26, we’ll spend three days discussing the Debris Avalanche. Succeeding days will cover the Pyroclastic Density Current (lateral pyroclastic surge), the Blast Pyroclastic Flows, the Plinian Column and ashfall, the Pumiceous Pyroclastic Flows, and the Lahars. Topics then turn to the new and affected lakes…Coldwater Lake, South Fork Castle Creek Lake, Spirit Lake, Saint Helens Lake, Fawn Lake, Venus Lake, and the other blast zone lakes. Plus, stories about superelevations of high-velocity lahars, the volcano’s missing glaciers, Crescent Ridge, and tales of friends and colleagues.
The subsequent summer and fall 1980 eruptions and spectacular pyroclastic flows out of the new amphitheater deserve their own slide shows too (May 25, June 12, July 22, August 7, and October 16-18).
