Day 82     June 9, 1980  Monday

USGS daily report
Steam plumes and some ash continue to be vented. No harmonic tremor was recorded today, and seismicity was low. When observers were able to see into the crater, they noticed a new, crescent-shaped lake in the northern part of the crater floor. It was about 1,000 feet long and 300 feet wide. On Friday June 13th, the moon will be at its closest proximity to the Earth since May 17th, and strong tides will result. Although geologists generally agree that tides, by themselves, cannot cause volcanic eruptions, some concede that strong Earth tides might be "the straw that breaks the camel's back" if the volcano is already primed for an eruption. 
Because volcano damage has been rare in this country, no one is certain how much of the private losses caused by the eruption will be covered by insurance. People who are listed as missing pose special problems for life insurance companies and claimants.   [USGS PP 1249]
